BURDEN OF PROOF
● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ●
CARD 1 · DEFINITION

What Is the Burden of Proof?

The burden of proof refers to the responsibility of a party in a legal proceeding to prove the facts necessary to establish their case.

The party who has the burden of proof must provide enough evidence to satisfy the applicable legal standard.

The burden of proof is an important part of the justice system because it determines who must prove a particular claim and how convincing the evidence must be.

The rules concerning burden of proof vary depending on the type of case and the legal system involved.

CARD 2 · CRIMINAL CASES

Burden of Proof in Criminal Cases

In criminal cases, the prosecution generally has the burden of proving that the accused committed the offence.

The accused is generally presumed innocent unless and until the prosecution proves guilt according to the applicable standard.

In many common law systems, the prosecution must prove guilt beyond a reasonable doubt.

This is a high standard because a criminal conviction can have serious consequences for the accused.

For example, if a person is accused of theft, the prosecution must present sufficient evidence to establish the elements of the offence to the required standard.

If the prosecution cannot satisfy that burden, the accused should not be convicted.

CARD 3 · PRESUMPTION OF INNOCENCE

Presumption of Innocence

The presumption of innocence is closely connected to the burden of proof.

It means that a person accused of a criminal offence is treated as innocent unless guilt is established according to law.

The prosecution therefore generally carries the burden of proving the accusation rather than requiring the accused to prove their innocence.

The presumption of innocence is an important protection for individuals facing criminal charges.
CARD 4 · CRIMINAL STANDARD

Beyond a Reasonable Doubt

Beyond a reasonable doubt is a high standard of proof commonly associated with criminal cases in common law legal systems.

It does not mean that the prosecution must prove its case with absolute or mathematical certainty.

Instead, the evidence must be sufficiently convincing that the required level of reasonable doubt about the accused's guilt has been overcome.

The exact wording and interpretation of this standard can vary between jurisdictions.
CARD 5 · CIVIL CASES

Burden of Proof in Civil Cases

The burden of proof in civil cases generally rests on the party bringing the claim, often called the claimant or plaintiff.

In many legal systems, the usual standard in civil cases is the balance of probabilities, sometimes described as proving that something is more likely than not to have occurred.

For example, if a person claims that a business breached a contract, the claimant generally has to establish the facts supporting that claim according to the applicable civil standard.

The civil burden of proof usually requires the claimant to establish the claim according to the applicable civil standard.
CARD 6 · BALANCE OF PROBABILITIES

Balance of Probabilities

The balance of probabilities is a common civil standard of proof.

Under this standard, the court generally decides whether a particular fact is more likely than not to be true.

This standard is lower than the criminal standard commonly used in criminal cases.

For example, if a court concludes that there is a greater than 50% likelihood that a particular event occurred, the fact may be established on the balance of probabilities, subject to the rules of the relevant legal system.

Balance of probabilities = Whether something is more likely than not to be true.
CARD 7 · LEGAL & EVIDENTIAL BURDEN

Legal Burden and Evidential Burden

Legal Burden

The legal burden is the ultimate responsibility to prove a particular issue to the required standard.

For example, in a criminal prosecution, the prosecution generally carries the legal burden of proving the accused's guilt.

Evidential Burden

The evidential burden concerns the responsibility to produce enough evidence to raise or support a particular issue.

For example, a party may need to introduce sufficient evidence before a court will consider a particular defence or argument.

The exact meaning and operation of these concepts varies between legal systems.
CARD 8 · BURDEN AND STANDARD

Burden of Proof vs. Standard of Proof

Burden of Proof

Who must prove the claim?

It identifies the party responsible for establishing the necessary facts.

Standard of Proof

How convincing must the proof be?

It describes the level of certainty required to satisfy the burden.

Burden of proof = Who must prove the claim.

Standard of proof = How convincing the proof must be.
CARD 9 · CRIMINAL VS. CIVIL

Different Standards in Criminal and Civil Cases

Criminal Cases

Commonly, the prosecution must prove guilt beyond a reasonable doubt.

This is generally a higher standard of proof.

Civil Cases

The party bringing the claim commonly proves it on the balance of probabilities.

This is generally a lower standard of proof.

The specific standards and terminology can differ between jurisdictions.

CARD 10 · EXAMPLE

Burden of Proof in Practice

Imagine that a person is accused of stealing a valuable item.

The prosecution claims that the accused committed the theft and presents evidence such as witness testimony and other relevant material.

The prosecution has the burden of proving the elements of the offence to the required criminal standard.

If the evidence does not satisfy the required standard, the court should not find the accused guilty merely because the accusation appears possible.

Civil Example

Now imagine a civil case in which a customer claims that a company failed to provide a service required by contract.

The customer generally carries the burden of proving the contractual claim to the applicable civil standard.

The different standards reflect the different purposes and consequences of criminal and civil proceedings.
CARD 11 · EVIDENCE & DEFENCES

Evidence, Defences and Presumptions

Burden of Proof and Evidence

Burden of proof is closely connected to the law of evidence.

  • Witness testimony.
  • Documents.
  • Physical evidence.
  • Digital records.
  • Expert evidence.
  • Photographs or recordings.

The court considers admissible evidence according to the relevant rules and determines whether the party carrying the burden has established the required facts.

Burden of Proof and Defences

The burden of proof can become more complicated when a defendant raises a legal defence.

The precise allocation of the burden depends on the type of defence and the law of the jurisdiction.

CARD 12 · PRESUMPTIONS & ACCESS

Presumptions and Access to Justice

Presumptions

A presumption is a legal rule that allows or requires a particular conclusion to be drawn from established facts unless the presumption is rebutted where the law permits this.

Presumptions can affect who must produce evidence on a particular issue.

For example, the presumption of innocence in criminal law means that the prosecution generally begins with the responsibility of proving guilt.

Access to Justice

The rules concerning burden of proof can have a significant effect on access to justice.

A person who has a valid legal claim may still be unsuccessful if they cannot provide sufficient evidence to satisfy the applicable standard.

Fair rules concerning evidence, disclosure, witnesses, and court procedures help provide a structured process for determining whether legal claims have been proved.
CARD 13 · FAIR TRIAL & KEY TAKEAWAY

Why the Burden of Proof Matters

The burden of proof helps:

  • Determine who must establish a particular fact.
  • Protect individuals from unsupported accusations.
  • Provide a consistent method for deciding disputed facts.
  • Distinguish between suspicion and legally sufficient proof.
  • Ensure that courts base decisions on evidence rather than speculation.
  • Support fairness in both criminal and civil proceedings.

Fair Trial Rights

The burden of proof is closely connected to the right to a fair trial.

In criminal proceedings, placing the burden of proving guilt on the prosecution helps protect accused persons from being required to establish their own innocence.

Fair procedures concerning evidence and proof also help ensure that courts reach decisions based on properly presented and tested evidence.

Burden of proof = Who must prove the claim.

Standard of proof = How convincing the proof must be.

In criminal cases, the prosecution generally carries the burden of proving guilt, while in civil cases the party bringing the claim generally carries the burden of proving it.
