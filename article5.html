Article 5 – Right to Liberty and Security

A structured legal note on the right to liberty and security, and the protection against arbitrary detention under the European Convention on Human Rights.

Definition

Article 5 of the European Convention on Human Rights protects the right to liberty and security of person. It is designed to prevent arbitrary detention and to ensure that no one is deprived of liberty unless the detention fits one of the limited situations allowed by the Convention and follows lawful procedure. The European Court of Human Rights has explained that Article 5 protects physical liberty, not every restriction on movement.

Legal Provision

Article 5 provides that everyone has the right to liberty and security, and that no one may be deprived of liberty except in the specific cases listed in Article 5(1) and in accordance with a procedure prescribed by law.

These lawful cases include detention after conviction, detention for disobeying a court order or securing a legal obligation, arrest on reasonable suspicion of an offence or to prevent an offence or escape, detention of a minor for educational supervision or to bring the minor before the competent authority, detention for public-health or similar reasons, and detention to prevent unlawful entry, deportation, or extradition.

Article 5 also requires prompt information on arrest reasons, prompt appearance before a judge in Article 5(1)(c) cases, speedy judicial review of detention, and a right to compensation where detention breaches the Article.

Purpose of Article 5

The purpose of Article 5 is to protect individual liberty by making sure detention is lawful, necessary, and not arbitrary. It also gives detained persons important safeguards, such as prompt information, judicial supervision, review of detention, and compensation if detention is unlawful. The Court has said that the aim of Article 5 is to protect people from arbitrary deprivation of liberty.

Key Principles

  • Protection from Arbitrary Detention – A person cannot be detained unless the case falls within Article 5(1) and the detention is lawful.
  • Physical Liberty – Article 5 concerns physical liberty, not every restriction on movement.
  • Lawfulness and Legal Certainty – Domestic law must be clear, precise, foreseeable, and compatible with the Convention.
  • Protection Against Arbitrariness – Even where domestic law allows detention, the detention must not be arbitrary.
  • Prompt Safeguards – A detained person must be informed promptly, brought promptly before a judge in Article 5(1)(c) cases, and able to challenge detention quickly.
  • Judicial Review – A court must be able to review the lawfulness of detention speedily.
  • Compensation – If detention breaches Article 5, the victim has an enforceable right to compensation.
  • Limited and Specific Exceptions – Detention is allowed only in the listed situations in Article 5(1).

Leading Cases

  • Engel and Others v. the Netherlands – Confirmed that Article 5 protects physical liberty and helps distinguish deprivation of liberty from mere restrictions on movement.
  • Winterwerp v. the Netherlands – Set important standards for the detention of persons of unsound mind under Article 5(1)(e).
  • McKay v. the United Kingdom – Explained that prompt and automatic judicial control is required after arrest and that the Article 5(3) safeguard is strict.
  • Saadi v. the United Kingdom – Confirmed that immigration detention must still be free from arbitrariness and closely connected to its lawful purpose.
  • Chahal v. the United Kingdom – Reinforced the importance of Article 5 safeguards in the context of detention linked to removal or security-related proceedings.

Key Legal Terms

  • Liberty and Security – The right not to be arbitrarily detained and to be protected during detention.
  • Deprivation of Liberty – A serious restriction on physical freedom amounting to detention.
  • Lawful Detention – Detention that fits one of the permitted Article 5 grounds and follows legal procedure.
  • Arbitrary Detention – Detention that is unfair, unforeseeable, or not justified by the Convention.
  • Prompt Judicial Review – Fast court supervision of detention to prevent abuse.
  • Compensation – Payment or remedy available where detention breaches Article 5.

Exam Tips

  • Remember that Article 5 is about liberty, security, and protection against arbitrary detention.
  • Mention the lawful detention grounds in Article 5(1).
  • Always include Article 5(2), (3), (4), and (5) because the Article is not just about detention but also about safeguards.
  • Use cases like Engel, Winterwerp, McKay, and Saadi to support your answer.

Summary

Article 5 protects one of the most important rights in the Convention: freedom from arbitrary detention. It allows deprivation of liberty only in specific lawful situations and requires strong safeguards such as prompt information, judicial control, speedy review, and compensation. Through case law, the European Court of Human Rights has made clear that liberty must be protected in a practical and effective way, not just in theory.
