Article 2 – Right to Life

A structured legal note on one of the most fundamental rights in the European Convention on Human Rights.

Definition

Definition

Article 2 of the European Convention on Human Rights protects the right to life and imposes duties on States to refrain from unlawful deprivation of life and to take reasonable measures to protect individuals from serious risks to life. This right is one of the most important of the Convention since without the right to life it is impossible to enjoy the other rights.

Legal Provision

Legal Provision

Article 2 states:

1. Everyone’s right to life shall be protected by law. No one shall be deprived of his life intentionally save in the execution of a sentence of a court following his conviction of a crime for which this penalty is provided by law.

2. Deprivation of life shall not be regarded as inflicted in contravention of this Article when it results from the use of force which is no more than absolutely necessary:

(a) in defence of any person from unlawful violence;
(b) in order to effect a lawful arrest or to prevent the escape of a person lawfully detained;

Purpose

Purpose of Article 2

The purpose of Article 2 is to protect one's right to life, which is the foundation for all other human rights. It requires States to respect and protect human life by preventing unlawful killings, taking reasonable steps to safeguard individuals whose lives are at real and immediate risk, and conducting effective investigations whenever a death occurs in suspicious circumstances or may involve State responsibility.

The ultimate aim of Article 2 is to ensure that every person's life is valued and protected under the law.

Key Principles

Key Principles

  • Protection of Life – States must not unlawfully or intentionally take life.
  • Positive Obligation – States must take reasonable steps to protect people whose lives are at real and immediate risk.
  • Effective Investigation – Suspicious deaths or deaths involving State responsibility must be investigated effectively.
  • Absolutely Necessary Force – Lethal force may only be used when absolutely necessary and proportionate.
  • Legal Protection – States must have laws and systems that effectively safeguard the right to life.
  • Strict Interpretation – Exceptions to Article 2 are narrowly interpreted because the right to life is fundamental.
Leading Cases

Leading Cases

  • McCann and Others v. the United Kingdom – Established that the use of lethal force must be absolutely necessary and strictly proportionate.
  • Osman v. the United Kingdom – Confirmed that States have positive obligations to protect individuals facing a real and immediate risk to life.
  • Kaya v. Turkey – Reinforced the duty to conduct effective investigations into deaths involving possible State responsibility.
  • Opuz v. Turkey – Held that a State may violate Article 2 by failing to protect victims of domestic violence when authorities knew or should have known of a real risk to life.

Key Legal Terms

  • Right to Life – The fundamental right of every person to have their life protected by law.
  • Positive Obligation – The State's duty to take reasonable measures to protect life.
  • Negative Obligation – The State's duty to refrain from unlawfully taking life.
  • Absolutely Necessary – The strict standard governing the lawful use of lethal force.
  • Proportionality – The force used must be proportionate to the legitimate aim pursued.
  • Effective Investigation – A prompt, independent, and thorough investigation into potentially unlawful deaths.
  • Real and Immediate Risk – A risk to life that the authorities knew or ought to have known about.

Exam Tips

  • Remember that Article 2 protects both against unlawful killing and requires States to protect life.
  • Distinguish between negative obligations (not to unlawfully take life) and positive obligations (to protect individuals from real and immediate risks).
  • Mention the duty to conduct an effective investigation when a death occurs in suspicious circumstances.
  • Support your answer with key cases such as McCann and Others v. the United Kingdom, Osman v. the United Kingdom, and Opuz v. Turkey where relevant.

Summary

Article 2 protects the most basic human right—the right to life. It not only prohibits unlawful killings by the State but also requires governments to actively protect life, investigate deaths effectively, and ensure that any use of lethal force is strictly necessary and proportionate. Through its case law, the European Court of Human Rights has interpreted Article 2 as imposing both negative and positive obligations on States, making it one of the strongest safeguards in the Convention.
