OFFER AND ACCEPTANCE
● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ●
CARD 1 · DEFINITION

What Are Offer and Acceptance?

Offer + Acceptance = Agreement

Offer and acceptance are fundamental elements of contract formation.

A contract is generally formed when one party makes an offer and the other party accepts that offer, provided that the other requirements for a valid contract are also satisfied.

Agreement alone is not necessarily enough to create an enforceable contract. Depending on the legal system, consideration, intention to create legal relations, capacity, certainty, and legality may also be relevant.

The central question is: Have the parties reached a legally binding agreement?

CARD 2 · OFFER

What Is an Offer?

An offer is a clear statement showing willingness to enter into a contract on specified terms, with the intention that it will become binding if accepted.

The person making the offer is called the offeror.

The person receiving the offer is called the offeree.

An offer should generally contain sufficiently clear terms so that the parties and a court can determine what agreement is being proposed.

Example

"I will sell you my bicycle for $200."

This may constitute an offer because it identifies the subject matter and price and indicates willingness to be bound if accepted.

CARD 3 · FEATURES OF AN OFFER

Essential Features of an Offer

An offer generally needs to demonstrate:

  • A clear willingness to contract.
  • Sufficiently certain terms.
  • An intention that acceptance will create a binding agreement.
  • Communication to the person or persons to whom it is made.

Intention to Be Bound

A statement made casually or socially may not constitute an offer.

For example, "I might sell my bicycle for $200" is less likely to be an offer because the language suggests uncertainty.

By contrast, "I will sell you my bicycle for $200" more clearly demonstrates an intention to contract.

The precise legal requirements depend on the jurisdiction and circumstances.

CARD 4 · CERTAINTY & COMMUNICATION

Certainty and Communication

Certainty of Terms

An offer must normally be sufficiently certain. Important terms may include:

  • Subject matter.
  • Price.
  • Quantity.
  • Identity of the parties.
  • Method of performance.
  • Timing of performance.

A court may refuse to enforce an alleged contract if its terms are too uncertain. However, courts in some legal systems may interpret or imply terms where appropriate.

Communication

An offer generally must be communicated to the offeree.

A person cannot ordinarily accept an offer they did not know existed.
CARD 5 · INVITATION TO TREAT

Offer vs. Invitation to Treat

An invitation to treat is generally an invitation for people to make offers rather than an offer itself.

Common examples include:

  • Goods displayed in shops.
  • Advertisements.
  • Catalogues.
  • Menus.
  • Auction advertisements.

Why Does It Matter?

The distinction determines who is actually making the offer and who has the power to accept it.

Offer = Proposal capable of acceptance.

Invitation to Treat = Invitation for others to make offers.
CARD 6 · SHOP DISPLAYS & ADVERTISEMENTS

Shop Displays & Advertisements

Goods Displayed in Shops

In many common-law systems, displaying goods in a shop is generally treated as an invitation to treat rather than an offer.

The customer may make the offer by presenting the item for purchase, while the seller may accept or reject it according to the applicable rules.

Advertisements

Advertisements are generally treated as invitations to treat.

For example:

"Laptop for sale — $500."

This usually invites customers to make offers. However, an advertisement can sometimes constitute an offer when its wording clearly demonstrates an intention to be legally bound.

CARD 7 · UNILATERAL & BILATERAL CONTRACTS

Unilateral and Bilateral Contracts

Unilateral Contract

A unilateral offer involves a promise in exchange for the completion of an act.

"I will pay you $100 if you find and return my missing phone."

Acceptance occurs through performance of the requested act rather than through a traditional promise to perform.

Bilateral Contract

A bilateral contract involves an exchange of promises.

For example, A promises to sell a computer to B for $800, while B promises to pay $800.

Most ordinary commercial contracts are bilateral.

CARD 8 · COUNTEROFFER & REJECTION

Counteroffer and Rejection

Counteroffer

A counteroffer occurs when the offeree responds to an offer by proposing different terms.

A: "I will sell you my bicycle for $500."

B: "I will buy it for $400."

B has generally made a counteroffer rather than accepting A's original offer.

Rejection

An offer can generally be terminated by rejection.

Once rejected, the offeree cannot normally later accept the original offer unless the offeror makes it available again.

CARD 9 · ACCEPTANCE

What Is Acceptance?

Acceptance is the offeree's final and unqualified agreement to the terms of the offer.

Acceptance generally requires:

  • Agreement with the terms of the offer.
  • An intention to accept.
  • Communication or conduct recognised by law as acceptance.
  • Compliance with any legally relevant prescribed method of acceptance.

Example

A: "I will sell you my bicycle for $300."

B: "I accept."

This normally demonstrates acceptance because B has agreed to the offer as made.

CARD 10 · MIRROR IMAGE RULE

Acceptance Must Correspond With the Offer

Acceptance generally must be unqualified. The offeree must accept the offer as it stands.

A: "I will sell you my laptop for $700."

B: "I accept for $600."

This is generally a counteroffer, not acceptance.

Mirror Image Rule

Traditional common-law contract law uses the mirror image rule, under which acceptance must correspond with the offer.

An acceptance that introduces new or different terms may therefore be treated as a counteroffer.

Modern legislation and case law may modify this principle in particular circumstances.

CARD 11 · SILENCE & CONDUCT

Silence and Acceptance by Conduct

Silence as Acceptance

As a general principle, silence alone does not normally constitute acceptance.

"If I do not hear from you, I will assume you agree."

A person cannot ordinarily create a contract simply by making such a statement.

Exceptions may arise depending on previous dealings, agreement, conduct, custom, and applicable law.

Acceptance by Conduct

Acceptance does not always have to be expressed in words. Conduct may demonstrate acceptance where it objectively shows agreement to the offer.

CARD 12 · REVOCATION & TERMINATION

Revocation and When an Offer Ends

Revocation

Revocation is the withdrawal of an offer by the offeror.

An offeror can generally revoke an offer before it is accepted, provided that the revocation is legally effective.

Communication of Revocation

Revocation generally must be communicated to the offeree. A secret decision to withdraw an offer will not normally be sufficient.

Other Ways an Offer May End

  • Acceptance.
  • Rejection.
  • Counteroffer.
  • Revocation.
  • Lapse of time.
  • Failure of a condition.
  • Death in certain circumstances.
  • Other events recognised by law.
CARD 13 · LAPSE, OPTIONS & UNILATERAL OFFERS

Special Rules on Ending Offers

Lapse of Time

An offer may expire after a specified deadline or after a reasonable period.

What is reasonable can depend on the subject matter, market conditions, and circumstances.

Option Contracts

An option contract involves a separate commitment to keep an offer open for a specified period.

Where the requirements for an enforceable option are satisfied, the offeror may be restricted from freely revoking the offer during that period.

Unilateral Offers

Where an offeree has begun performance of the requested act, some legal systems may restrict revocation in certain circumstances.

CARD 14 · ELECTRONIC CONTRACTS

Electronic Acceptance

Modern contracts are frequently formed electronically.

Acceptance may occur through:

  • Email.
  • Online forms.
  • Electronic signatures.
  • Clicking an "I agree" button.
  • Other forms of digital communication.

Acceptance by Email

Where contracts are negotiated through email, questions may arise about when acceptance becomes effective.

Relevant factors can include when the acceptance was sent, when it was received, the parties' agreement, the communication system, and applicable electronic-transactions legislation.

Clickwrap Agreements

A clickwrap agreement generally requires a user to actively indicate agreement to contractual terms.

Courts may consider whether the terms were reasonably presented and whether the user had a meaningful opportunity to review them.

CARD 15 · KEY TAKEAWAY

Key Takeaway

Offer = A definite proposal capable of acceptance.

Acceptance = Final agreement to the offer's terms.

Counteroffer = A new proposal with different terms.

Invitation to Treat = An invitation for others to make offers.

Revocation = Withdrawal of an offer before effective acceptance.

Offer and acceptance help determine whether an agreement exists, when a contract was formed, and what terms were agreed.

However, offer and acceptance alone may not establish an enforceable contract. Depending on the legal system, consideration, intention to create legal relations, capacity, certainty, and legality may also be required.

The objective approach is important: courts commonly ask what a reasonable person would understand from the parties' words and conduct rather than relying solely on secret intentions.
