Article 3 – Prohibition of Torture

A structured legal note on the absolute prohibition of torture and inhuman or degrading treatment under the European Convention on Human Rights.

Definition

Article 3 of the European Convention on Human Rights protects everyone from torture and from inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment. It is one of the most important rights in the Convention because it is absolute, meaning it cannot be limited or suspended under any circumstances.

Legal Provision

Article 3 states:

"No one shall be subjected to torture or to inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment."

Purpose of Article 3

The purpose of Article 3 is to protect human dignity and physical and mental integrity. It ensures that every person is treated with respect, regardless of who they are or what they have done.

The ultimate aim of Article 3 is to ensure that no person is subjected to treatment that causes serious physical or psychological suffering or seriously violates their human dignity.

Key Principles

  • Absolute Right – Article 3 is absolute and cannot be restricted or suspended, even during war or a public emergency.
  • Prohibition of Torture – States must never subject individuals to torture or other prohibited forms of ill-treatment.
  • Positive Obligation – States must take reasonable measures to protect individuals from serious ill-treatment, including abuse by private individuals.
  • Effective Investigation – Authorities must conduct an effective investigation when there is a credible allegation of torture or serious ill-treatment.
  • Human Dignity – Article 3 protects individuals from treatment that seriously violates their physical or psychological integrity and dignity.
  • No Balancing of Interests – A State cannot justify torture or prohibited ill-treatment by relying on public safety, national security, or other competing interests.

Types of Treatment

  • Torture – The most severe form of ill-treatment, involving intentional acts that cause very serious physical or mental suffering.
  • Inhuman Treatment – Treatment that causes intense physical or psychological suffering but does not reach the level of torture.
  • Degrading Treatment – Treatment that humiliates or seriously diminishes a person's dignity, causing feelings of fear, anguish, or inferiority.

Leading Cases

  • Soering v. the United Kingdom – The Court held that extraditing a person to face the death row phenomenon could expose them to treatment contrary to Article 3.
  • Tyrer v. the United Kingdom – The Court found that judicial corporal punishment constituted degrading treatment contrary to Article 3.
  • Selmouni v. France – The Court found that severe police abuse amounted to torture and confirmed that the Convention's understanding of torture can develop over time.
  • A. v. the United Kingdom – The Court found a violation of Article 3 where the State failed to provide adequate protection against serious child abuse.
  • M.C. v. Bulgaria – The Court recognised positive obligations concerning the effective investigation and legal protection of individuals against serious sexual violence.

Key Legal Terms

  • Absolute Right – A right that cannot be restricted or suspended under any circumstances.
  • Torture – Deliberate treatment causing very serious physical or mental suffering.
  • Inhuman Treatment – Treatment causing serious physical or psychological suffering.
  • Degrading Treatment – Treatment that humiliates or seriously diminishes a person's dignity.
  • Positive Obligation – The State's duty to take reasonable measures to protect individuals from serious ill-treatment.
  • Effective Investigation – A prompt, independent, thorough, and effective investigation into credible allegations of serious ill-treatment.
  • Human Dignity – The inherent worth and respect owed to every person.

Exam Tips

  • Remember that Article 3 is an absolute right.
  • Know the difference between torture, inhuman treatment, and degrading treatment.
  • Be able to explain the State's positive obligations under Article 3.
  • Remember that Article 3 can require States to protect individuals from serious ill-treatment by private individuals.
  • Mention the duty to conduct an effective investigation when there is a credible allegation of serious ill-treatment.
  • Support your answer with leading cases such as Soering, Tyrer, Selmouni, and A. v. the United Kingdom where relevant.

Summary

Article 3 provides absolute protection against torture and inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment. It requires States not only to avoid such conduct themselves but also to protect individuals from serious ill-treatment, investigate credible allegations effectively, and ensure respect for human dignity. Through its case law, the European Court of Human Rights has developed Article 3 into one of the strongest protections against serious ill-treatment in the Convention.
