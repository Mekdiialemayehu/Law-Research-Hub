JUDICIAL REVIEW
● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ●
CARD 1 · DEFINITION

What Is Judicial Review?

Judicial review is the process by which courts examine whether a decision, action, or law is lawful.

It is particularly important when courts review the actions of government bodies and public authorities.

Judicial review helps ensure that public authorities act within the powers given to them by law and follow the legal procedures that apply to them.

The exact meaning and scope of judicial review vary between legal systems.

CARD 2 · PURPOSE

Purpose of Judicial Review

The main purpose of judicial review is to ensure that public power is exercised lawfully, fairly, and within legal limits.

Courts may review whether a public authority:

  • Had the legal power to make a decision.
  • Followed the correct legal procedure.
  • Took relevant factors into account.
  • Acted fairly.
  • Exercised its powers for a proper purpose.
  • Respected applicable legal rights.
Judicial review is therefore an important part of the Rule of Law because it helps prevent public authorities from exercising power arbitrarily or unlawfully.
CARD 3 · PUBLIC AUTHORITIES

Who Can Be Subject to Judicial Review?

Judicial review commonly concerns decisions made by public authorities, such as:

  • Government ministries.
  • Local government authorities.
  • Regulatory agencies.
  • Public officials.
  • Administrative tribunals.
  • Other bodies exercising public functions.

Whether a particular organisation can be reviewed depends on the legal system and the nature of the power it is exercising.

The central question is generally whether the body is exercising a public power that is legally subject to judicial supervision.
CARD 4 · GROUNDS

Grounds of Judicial Review

1. Illegality

A public authority must act within the powers given to it by law.

If an authority goes beyond its legal powers, its decision may be unlawful.

This is sometimes described using the Latin term ultra vires, meaning "beyond the powers."

2. Procedural Unfairness

Public authorities are generally required to follow legally required procedures and, in appropriate circumstances, act fairly.

A decision may be challenged if the authority failed to follow an important procedural requirement.

CARD 5 · REASONABLENESS & PROPORTIONALITY

Unreasonableness and Proportionality

Unreasonableness

In some legal systems, courts may review a decision that is so unreasonable that it falls outside the range of decisions that a lawful public authority could properly make.

The precise standard used by courts differs between jurisdictions.

Proportionality

In some legal systems, particularly in cases involving fundamental rights, courts may consider whether a government measure was proportionate to the legitimate objective it was intended to achieve.

Proportionality can involve examining whether:

  • The measure pursued a legitimate purpose.
  • The measure was suitable for achieving that purpose.
  • A less restrictive measure could have been used.
  • The effect on individual rights was justified in the circumstances.
CARD 6 · LEGALITY VS. MERITS

Judicial Review and the Merits of a Decision

Judicial review is generally concerned with the legality of a decision, rather than simply whether the decision was the best or most sensible one.

Imagine that a government department has the legal authority to choose between several possible policies.

A court conducting judicial review may examine whether the department acted lawfully when making its choice.

The court will not necessarily replace the department's policy judgment with its own simply because the judges might have preferred a different policy.

This helps maintain a distinction between the roles of courts and public authorities.

CARD 7 · EXAMPLE

Judicial Review in Practice

Imagine that a local government authority refuses to grant a permit.

The person believes the authority made the decision without having the legal power to do so and failed to follow the required procedure.

The person may seek judicial review.

The court could examine:

  • Whether the authority had legal authority to make the decision.
  • Whether the correct procedure was followed.
  • Whether the authority acted within the limits of its powers.
If the court finds that the decision was unlawful, it may provide an appropriate legal remedy.
CARD 8 · REMEDIES

Remedies in Judicial Review

The remedies available in judicial review depend on the legal system.

Possible remedies can include:

  • Quashing an unlawful decision — removing the legal effect of the decision.
  • Ordering a public authority to perform a legal duty.
  • Prohibiting a public authority from acting unlawfully.
  • Making a declaration about the legal rights or position of the parties.

In some circumstances, other forms of relief may also be available.

The purpose of a remedy is generally to address the legal problem identified by the court.
CARD 9 · CONSTITUTION & HUMAN RIGHTS

Judicial Review, Constitutional Law & Human Rights

Constitutional Law

Judicial review can be closely connected to constitutional law.

In some countries, courts have authority to review legislation to determine whether it is compatible with the constitution.

However, not every legal system gives courts the same power to review legislation.

Human Rights

Judicial review can also play an important role in protecting human rights.

Courts may be able to examine whether a public authority acted consistently with applicable human rights protections.

  • Freedom of expression.
  • Freedom from discrimination.
  • The right to privacy.
  • Freedom from arbitrary detention.
  • The right to a fair hearing.
CARD 10 · SEPARATION OF POWERS

Judicial Review and Separation of Powers

Judicial review is closely connected to the principle of separation of powers.

Legislature

Creates and changes laws.

Executive

Implements and administers laws.

Judiciary

Interprets and applies the law and may review certain exercises of public power.

Judicial review allows courts to ensure that public authorities remain within the legal limits imposed on them.

At the same time, courts must respect the responsibilities assigned to other branches of government.

Judicial review therefore involves a balance between controlling unlawful government action and respecting the proper role of public institutions.
CARD 11 · APPEAL & LIMITATIONS

Judicial Review vs. Appeal

Appeal

Usually involves asking a higher court to reconsider a decision made by a lower court.

Question: "Did the lower court make the correct legal decision?"

Judicial Review

Commonly involves examining whether a public authority or other body acted lawfully.

Question: "Did the public authority act within its legal powers and follow the law?"

Limitations

  • Which bodies can be reviewed.
  • Which decisions can be challenged.
  • The time allowed to bring a claim.
  • The legal grounds on which a challenge can be made.
  • The remedies a court can provide.
CARD 12 · KEY TAKEAWAY

Why Judicial Review Matters

Judicial review helps:

  • Protect the Rule of Law.
  • Prevent abuse of public power.
  • Ensure that public authorities remain within their legal powers.
  • Protect individuals from unlawful government decisions.
  • Promote lawful and fair administration.
  • Provide a mechanism for challenging certain government actions.
Judicial Review = Judicial examination of the legality of public power.

Judicial review is an important safeguard against unlawful government action. It helps ensure that public authorities exercise their powers within the limits established by law while respecting the rights of individuals.
