Selmouni v. France (1999)

European Court of Human Rights | Human Rights Law

Case Overview

Case Name: Selmouni v. France (1999)

Court: European Court of Human Rights (Grand Chamber)

Date: 28 July 1999

Area of Law: Human Rights Law / Prohibition of Torture and Ill-Treatment

Key Articles: Article 3 and Article 6 § 1 of the European Convention on Human Rights.

Key Principle: Severe police ill-treatment can amount to torture under Article 3. The Convention is a living instrument and must be interpreted in the light of present-day conditions and evolving standards of human rights protection.

Facts of the Case

Ahmed Selmouni was arrested and held in police custody in Bobigny, France, from 25 to 29 November 1991 in connection with drug-trafficking proceedings.

During his detention, Selmouni was examined several times by doctors. Medical certificates were prepared and recorded injuries that he had suffered.

Selmouni later complained that police officers had subjected him to serious physical and psychological ill-treatment while he was in custody.

The domestic proceedings concerning the police officers were lengthy. Criminal proceedings were eventually brought against them, and the Versailles Criminal Court convicted the officers in March 1999. The Court of Appeal later gave its judgment in July 1999.

Procedural History

Selmouni complained about the treatment he had suffered while in police custody and pursued proceedings before the French authorities.

After the domestic proceedings, he brought his complaint before the European Court of Human Rights. He argued that the treatment he had suffered violated Article 3 and that the length of the proceedings violated Article 6 § 1.

The European Court of Human Rights delivered its Grand Chamber judgment on 28 July 1999.

Key Legal Issue

Whether the treatment inflicted on Selmouni while he was in police custody was sufficiently serious to constitute torture or inhuman or degrading treatment under Article 3 of the European Convention on Human Rights.

The Court also had to determine whether the length of the domestic proceedings exceeded the reasonable-time requirement under Article 6 § 1.

Legal Arguments

Selmouni's Arguments

  • The treatment he suffered while in police custody was sufficiently severe to constitute a violation of Article 3.
  • The violence inflicted upon him by police officers amounted to torture rather than merely inhuman or degrading treatment.
  • The domestic proceedings had taken too long and therefore violated his right to a hearing within a reasonable time under Article 6 § 1.

France's Arguments

The French Government raised objections concerning the admissibility of the complaint, including whether domestic remedies had been exhausted. It also disputed the applicant's allegations and the assessment of the treatment under Article 3.

Legal Question Reformulated

The Court had to decide whether the treatment suffered by Selmouni during police custody reached the threshold of torture or inhuman or degrading treatment under Article 3, and whether the domestic proceedings had exceeded a reasonable time under Article 6 § 1.

Judgment and Reasoning

Decision

The European Court of Human Rights found that the treatment suffered by Selmouni amounted to torture and therefore violated Article 3 of the European Convention on Human Rights.

The Court also found a violation of Article 6 § 1 because the length of the domestic proceedings had exceeded a reasonable time.

  • Article 3: The Court found that the treatment inflicted on Selmouni by police officers was sufficiently serious to be classified as torture.
  • Article 6 § 1: The Court held that the proceedings had lasted longer than could reasonably be justified and therefore violated the applicant's right to a hearing within a reasonable time.

Key Judicial Reasoning

The Court considered the medical evidence together with Selmouni's account of what had happened during his detention. It concluded that the evidence demonstrated that he had suffered serious violence while under police control.

The Court emphasised that Article 3 protects one of the most fundamental values of democratic society: respect for human dignity. Treatment must be assessed according to the circumstances of the particular case, including its severity and duration.

Importantly, the Court held that treatment that might previously have been classified as inhuman or degrading could, as standards of human rights protection develop, be classified as torture.

The Court therefore described the Convention as a living instrument which must be interpreted in the light of present-day conditions. This allows the protection offered by Article 3 to develop as society's understanding of human dignity and unacceptable treatment evolves.

Living Instrument Principle

One of the most important aspects of Selmouni v. France is the Court's application of the "living instrument" approach.

Under this approach, the European Convention on Human Rights is not interpreted as a completely fixed document. Its meaning can develop in response to changing social conditions, values, and standards of human rights protection.

The principle was particularly important in Selmouni because it allowed the Court to recognise that conduct which might previously have been classified as inhuman or degrading treatment could now reach the higher threshold of torture.

Legal Impact

  • Strengthened the absolute prohibition of torture under Article 3 of the European Convention on Human Rights.
  • Established that serious police brutality can amount to torture even when committed during an official investigation or period of police custody.
  • Confirmed that the severity of treatment must be assessed according to contemporary human rights standards.
  • Strengthened the Court's "living instrument" approach to interpreting the Convention.
  • Demonstrated that States can be held responsible for serious ill-treatment carried out by public officials.
  • Became an important authority in later cases concerning torture, police violence, and detention conditions.

Broader Social Impact

  • Strengthened the understanding that people in State custody must be treated with dignity.
  • Raised the legal significance of police violence and abuse of power.
  • Reinforced the principle that torture cannot be justified by the circumstances of a criminal investigation.
  • Demonstrated the importance of independent investigations and accountability when allegations of police abuse arise.
  • Contributed to the development of stronger European human rights standards concerning treatment in custody.

Critical Evaluation

Strengths

  • The judgment gave strong protection to the absolute prohibition of torture.
  • It recognised that police officers exercising State authority must remain subject to human rights law.
  • The living-instrument approach allowed Article 3 to develop alongside modern understandings of human dignity.
  • The Court's reliance on medical evidence demonstrated the importance of objective evidence when assessing allegations of ill-treatment.

Criticisms

  • The distinction between torture and other forms of prohibited ill-treatment can sometimes be difficult to apply consistently.
  • The living-instrument approach can create debate about how far courts should develop the meaning of rights beyond their historical interpretation.
  • A judgment from an international court cannot by itself prevent future cases of police abuse; effective implementation at the national level remains necessary.

Overall Assessment: Selmouni v. France was a major development in Article 3 case law. By classifying serious police violence as torture and emphasising evolving standards of human rights protection, the Court strengthened the protection of human dignity and reinforced the absolute nature of the prohibition of torture.

Final Conclusion

Selmouni v. France remains a landmark case concerning torture, police ill-treatment, and the interpretation of Article 3 of the European Convention on Human Rights.

The Court found that the treatment suffered by Selmouni was sufficiently severe to constitute torture and that the domestic proceedings had also exceeded a reasonable time under Article 6 § 1.

Most importantly, the judgment demonstrated that human rights law must be capable of developing as society's understanding of dignity and unacceptable treatment changes. The living-instrument principle therefore became one of the most significant aspects of the case.

Personal Reflection

I found Selmouni v. France particularly important because it shows how human rights law can protect people from abuse by the State itself. A person in police custody is in a position where they depend on State authorities for their safety, which makes accountability especially important.

What interested me most about this case was the living-instrument principle. It demonstrates that the law does not have to remain limited to the standards of the past and can develop as society's understanding of human dignity changes.

This case also showed me why the prohibition of torture must be absolute. Even when someone is suspected of committing a crime, the State still has a responsibility to respect their fundamental human rights and dignity.
