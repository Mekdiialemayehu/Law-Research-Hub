Article 11 – Freedom of Peaceful Assembly and Association

A structured legal note on the freedom of peaceful assembly, freedom of association, and the protection of collective participation under the European Convention on Human Rights.

ECHR Article 11

Definition

Article 11 of the European Convention on Human Rights protects the freedom of peaceful assembly and the freedom of association with others, including the right to form and join trade unions. It is a qualified right, which means it can be restricted only in limited circumstances and only if the restriction is lawful, pursues a legitimate aim, and is necessary in a democratic society.

The European Court of Human Rights has recognised that peaceful assembly is a fundamental right in a democratic society and that Article 11 should not be interpreted narrowly.

Legal Provision

Legal Provision

Article 11 provides that everyone has the right to freedom of peaceful assembly and freedom of association with others, including the right to form and join trade unions for the protection of their interests.

Restrictions may be imposed only if they are prescribed by law and are necessary in a democratic society in the interests of national security or public safety, for the prevention of disorder or crime, for the protection of health or morals, or for the protection of the rights and freedoms of others.

The Article also allows lawful restrictions on the exercise of these rights by members of the armed forces, the police, and the State administration.

Purpose

Purpose of Article 11

The purpose of Article 11 is to protect collective action, democratic participation, and the ability of people to come together for shared aims. It protects public protest, peaceful demonstration, trade union activity, and the right of individuals to associate freely without unfair State interference.

The Court has also recognised that Article 11 is closely linked to Article 10 because peaceful assembly is often a way of expressing opinions and taking part in public debate.

Key Principles

Key Principles

  • Peaceful Assembly – The Article protects gatherings that are peaceful in nature. A demonstration does not lose protection simply because there is some sporadic disorder by others, so long as the individual concerned remains peaceful.
  • Freedom of Association – People have the right to join with others for a common purpose, including political, social, religious, and trade union aims.
  • Trade Union Protection – Article 11 specifically protects the right to form and join trade unions.
  • Negative Freedom of Association – Article 11 can also protect the right not to be forced to join a trade union in certain circumstances.
  • Qualified Right – The right can be restricted only under the strict conditions in Article 11(2).
  • Lawfulness – Any restriction must have a basis in law that is accessible and foreseeable.
  • Legitimate Aim – Restrictions must pursue one of the legitimate aims listed in the Convention.
  • Necessity and Proportionality – Even where there is a lawful aim, the State must show that the interference is proportionate and necessary in a democratic society.
  • Democratic Importance – Article 11 is central to democracy because it protects protest, political participation, and collective action.
Case Law

Leading Cases

  • Djavit An v. Turkey – Confirmed the importance of peaceful assembly and is often cited on the scope of Article 11 and the principle that the right should not be interpreted narrowly.
  • Ezelin v. France – Held that a person does not lose Article 11 protection simply because others in a demonstration act improperly, if that person remains peaceful.
  • Kudrevičius and Others v. Lithuania [GC] – Clarified when an assembly is protected by Article 11 and emphasised that restrictions must still be assessed under proportionality, even where a demonstration causes disruption.
  • Young, James and Webster v. the United Kingdom – A leading case on freedom of association and the concept of voluntary membership.
  • Sørensen and Rasmussen v. Denmark – Confirmed that forcing a person to join a trade union can violate the negative aspect of freedom of association.
  • Refah Partisi (The Welfare Party) and Others v. Turkey – A major case concerning political parties and the protection of democratic values under Article 11.
  • Oya Ataman v. Turkey – Demonstrated that the absence of prior authorisation does not automatically remove Article 11 protection from a peaceful assembly.

Key Legal Terms

  • Peaceful Assembly – A gathering of people that does not involve violent intentions.
  • Freedom of Association – The right to form or join groups and organisations for common purposes.
  • Trade Union – An organisation formed to protect workers’ interests.
  • Interference – Any State action that limits the exercise of Article 11 rights.
  • Prescribed by Law – A restriction must have a clear legal basis.
  • Necessary in a Democratic Society – A test used to determine whether a restriction is proportionate and justified.
  • Proportionality – The State must not go further than necessary to achieve its legitimate aim.

Exam Tips

  • Remember that Article 11 protects both peaceful assembly and association.
  • Explain whether the gathering was peaceful and whether the association was voluntary.
  • Mention that Article 11 is a qualified right, so restrictions may be allowed if they are lawful and proportionate.
  • Use cases such as Djavit An, Ezelin, Kudrevičius, Young, James and Webster, and Sørensen and Rasmussen to support your answer.

Summary

Article 11 protects one of the most important rights in a democratic society: the freedom to gather, protest peacefully, and associate with others for common goals. It also protects trade union activity and the right not to be forced into association in some situations.

Through its case law, the European Court of Human Rights has shown that this right is essential for democracy, public debate, and collective participation.
