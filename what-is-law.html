WHAT IS LAW?

CARD 1 · DEFINITION

What Is Law?

Law is a system of rules and principles created and recognised by a society or authority to regulate behaviour, protect rights, resolve disputes, and maintain order.

Laws establish what individuals, organisations, and governments are permitted or required to do. They can also establish consequences when legal rules are broken.

Law is not limited to criminal offences. It covers many areas of society, including contracts, property, family relationships, employment, human rights, businesses, and the relationship between individuals and the State.

CARD 2 · PURPOSE OF LAW

Purpose of Law

Law serves several important purposes in society.

1. Maintaining Order

Laws establish rules that help society function peacefully. Criminal laws, for example, prohibit conduct such as theft, violence, and fraud.

2. Protecting Rights

Law protects important rights and freedoms, including rights relating to life, privacy, equality, expression, and property.

3. Resolving Disputes

Courts and other legal institutions provide mechanisms for resolving disagreements between individuals, organisations, and governments.

4. Regulating Behaviour

Law establishes standards of behaviour and creates legal consequences when those standards are not followed.

5. Limiting Government Power

Constitutional and human rights laws can place limits on what governments and public authorities are allowed to do.

CARD 3 · KEY FEATURES

Key Features of Law

Law generally has several characteristics:

  • It creates rules that regulate behaviour.
  • It can impose rights and duties.
  • It provides mechanisms for resolving disputes.
  • It can be enforced through courts and other legal institutions.
  • It applies within particular legal jurisdictions.
  • It can change as society and legal institutions develop.
CARD 4 · EXAMPLE

Law in Practice

Suppose a person enters into a contract to buy a computer from another person. If the seller receives payment but refuses to provide the computer, the buyer may have a legal remedy.

The law provides rules governing contracts and can allow the dispute to be resolved through legal procedures.

Similarly, if someone commits theft, criminal law may provide a punishment because the conduct violates a rule established by the State.

These examples demonstrate that law regulates both relationships between private individuals and conduct considered harmful to society.
CARD 5 · AREAS OF LAW

Different Areas of Law

Law can be divided into many different areas.

Criminal Law

Criminal law deals with conduct considered an offence against society or the State.

Examples include theft, assault, fraud, and murder.

Civil Law

Civil law generally concerns disputes between individuals or organisations.

Examples include contract disputes, negligence claims, and property disputes.

Public Law

Public law regulates the relationship between individuals and the State.

Constitutional law, administrative law, and human rights law are examples of public law.

Private Law

Private law regulates relationships between individuals and organisations.

Contract law, tort law, and property law are examples of private law.

CARD 6 · IMPORTANCE

Why Law Matters

Law affects almost every part of everyday life. People interact with legal rules when they attend school, work, purchase goods, sign contracts, use property, participate in elections, or interact with government authorities.

Law also provides a framework for protecting individuals from abuses of power and for holding people and institutions accountable.

However, laws themselves can be criticised, challenged, changed, or declared incompatible with higher legal standards depending on the legal system.

Studying law involves more than memorising rules. It also involves examining how rules are created, interpreted, applied, and changed.

CARD 8 · KEY TAKEAWAY

Key Takeaway

Law is a system of rules and principles that regulates behaviour, protects rights, resolves disputes, and establishes the powers and responsibilities of individuals and institutions.

Understanding the purpose and structure of law provides the foundation for studying more complex legal concepts.
