Article 9 – Freedom of Thought, Conscience and Religion

A structured legal note on freedom of thought, conscience, and religion under the European Convention on Human Rights.

Definition

Article 9 of the European Convention on Human Rights protects the freedom of thought, conscience, and religion. It includes the freedom to change religion or belief and the freedom, alone or with others and in public or private, to manifest religion or belief through worship, teaching, practice, and observance. Article 9 protects both inner belief and outward expression, although the manifestation of religion or belief may be limited in limited and lawful circumstances.

Legal Provision

Article 9 provides that everyone has the right to freedom of thought, conscience and religion. This right includes freedom to change religion or belief and freedom, either alone or in community with others and in public or private, to manifest religion or belief in worship, teaching, practice and observance.

It also states that freedom to manifest religion or belief may be subject only to such limitations as are prescribed by law and are necessary in a democratic society in the interests of public safety, public order, health or morals, or for the protection of the rights and freedoms of others.

This means that the inner freedom to hold a belief is protected strongly, while the outward manifestation of that belief may be restricted if the restriction is lawful, pursues a legitimate aim, and is proportionate.

Purpose of Article 9

The purpose of Article 9 is to protect a person’s inner freedom of belief and the outward expression of that belief. It safeguards the right to hold religious or non-religious convictions, to change them, and to manifest them in daily life. The European Court of Human Rights has also recognised that the State must remain neutral and impartial when regulating freedom of religion.

Key Principles

  • Freedom of Thought and Conscience – A person has the right to hold beliefs without interference.
  • Freedom of Religion or Belief – Article 9 protects both religious belief and non-religious conviction.
  • Freedom to Change Religion – Individuals are free to change their religion or belief.
  • Manifestation of Religion – Beliefs may be expressed through worship, teaching, practice, observance, clothing, or symbols in some circumstances.
  • Qualified Right – Only the manifestation of religion can be limited, and only under Article 9(2).
  • Lawfulness – Any interference must be based on law.
  • Legitimate Aim – Restrictions must pursue one of the aims listed in Article 9(2).
  • Necessity and Proportionality – The interference must be necessary in a democratic society and proportionate to the aim pursued.
  • State Neutrality – The State must act as a neutral and impartial organiser of different religions and beliefs.

Leading Cases

  • Kokkinakis v. Greece – A leading case on Article 9, recognising the importance of freedom of religion and the limits on interference by the State.
  • Lautsi v. Italy – Considered Article 9 and the role of religious symbols in public life and education.
  • Eweida and Others v. the United Kingdom – Confirmed that wearing a visible religious symbol at work can fall within Article 9 and must be balanced against the rights of others and workplace interests.
  • Leyla Şahin v. Turkey – A major case on religious dress and restrictions on manifestation of belief.
  • S.A.S. v. France – Confirmed that Article 9 protects manifestation of religion, while also recognising that restrictions may be justified in limited circumstances.
  • Ossewaarde v. Russia – Concerned Article 9 and interference with religious meetings in a private home.

Key Legal Terms

  • Freedom of Thought – The right to hold ideas and convictions freely.
  • Conscience – A person’s moral beliefs and inner judgment.
  • Religion or Belief – Religious faith or sincerely held convictions, including non-religious beliefs in some situations.
  • Manifestation – The outward expression of belief through actions, symbols, worship, teaching, or observance.
  • Qualified Right – A right that can be limited in certain circumstances.
  • Neutrality and Impartiality – The State should not favour one belief over another.
  • Proportionality – The interference must not go beyond what is necessary.

Exam Tips

  • Remember that Article 9 protects both inner belief and outward manifestation.
  • Explain that Article 9 is a qualified right, so the manifestation of religion can be limited in some cases.
  • Always ask whether there was an interference, whether it was lawful, whether it had a legitimate aim, and whether it was necessary and proportionate.
  • Use cases such as Kokkinakis, Eweida, Leyla Şahin, and S.A.S. to support your answer.

Summary

Article 9 protects one of the most personal freedoms in the Convention: the right to think, believe, and worship freely. It safeguards both the private holding of beliefs and the public expression of them, while allowing limited restrictions on manifestation where they are lawful and proportionate. Through its case law, the European Court of Human Rights has made Article 9 an important protection for freedom of religion, conscience, and belief in a democratic society.
