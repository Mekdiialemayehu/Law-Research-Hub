JURISDICTION
● ● ● ● ● ● ● ●
CARD 1 · DEFINITION

What Is Jurisdiction?

Jurisdiction refers to the legal authority of a court or other legal institution to hear and decide a particular case.

A court does not automatically have the power to decide every legal dispute. Its jurisdiction depends on factors such as the type of case, the location of the parties or events, and the legal authority given to that court.

The concept of jurisdiction helps determine which court has the legal power to hear a case and make a binding decision.

CARD 2 · TYPES OF JURISDICTION

Types of Jurisdiction

Jurisdiction can be divided into several important categories.

  • Subject-Matter Jurisdiction
  • Personal Jurisdiction
  • Territorial Jurisdiction
  • Original Jurisdiction
  • Appellate Jurisdiction
  • Exclusive Jurisdiction
  • Concurrent Jurisdiction

Subject-Matter Jurisdiction

Subject-matter jurisdiction refers to a court's authority to hear a particular type of legal dispute.

Different courts may have authority over different areas of law, such as criminal cases, civil disputes, family matters, constitutional questions, or administrative disputes.

Personal Jurisdiction

Personal jurisdiction refers to a court's legal authority over the parties involved in a case.

A court may have personal jurisdiction over a defendant because the defendant lives, works, conducts business, or has another legally recognised connection within that jurisdiction.

CARD 3 · TERRITORIAL AND COURT JURISDICTION

Where and Which Court?

Territorial Jurisdiction

Territorial jurisdiction concerns the geographical area within which a court has authority.

A court may have jurisdiction over cases arising within a particular city, district, state or region, or country.

The rules governing territorial jurisdiction vary between legal systems.

Original Jurisdiction

Original jurisdiction refers to the authority of a court to hear a case at its initial stage.

A court exercising original jurisdiction is the court where the case begins rather than a court reviewing a decision made by another court.

Appellate Jurisdiction

Appellate jurisdiction is the authority of a higher court to review a decision made by a lower court.

An appellate court generally examines whether legal or procedural errors may have occurred rather than conducting the case in exactly the same way as the original trial court.

CARD 4 · EXCLUSIVE AND CONCURRENT JURISDICTION

Exclusive vs. Concurrent Jurisdiction

Exclusive Jurisdiction

Exclusive jurisdiction exists when a particular court or legal system has the sole authority to decide a certain type of case.

Other courts may therefore be prevented from hearing the same matter.

Concurrent Jurisdiction

Concurrent jurisdiction exists when more than one court or legal system has legal authority to hear the same type of dispute.

In such circumstances, the rules of the relevant legal system may determine which court is appropriate.

Exclusive jurisdiction means one court or legal system has the authority. Concurrent jurisdiction means more than one court or legal system may have authority.
CARD 5 · EXAMPLE

Jurisdiction in Practice

Imagine that a person living in Ethiopia enters into a contract with a company based in another country.

The company later fails to perform the contract, and the person wants to bring a legal claim.

Several jurisdictional questions may arise:

  • Which country's courts have authority over the dispute?
  • Which court has jurisdiction over contract claims?
  • Does the defendant have a sufficient connection with that country?
  • Where did the contract arise or where was it performed?
  • Does the contract contain a clause selecting a particular court or country?

These questions may need to be considered before determining where the case should be brought.

CARD 6 · CRIMINAL AND INTERNATIONAL JURISDICTION

Jurisdiction Beyond One Court

Jurisdiction in Criminal Cases

Jurisdiction is also important in criminal law.

A country will generally exercise criminal jurisdiction over offences committed within its territory.

However, legal systems can recognise other forms of jurisdiction in certain circumstances. A country's laws may provide jurisdiction over certain conduct committed outside its territory where particular legal conditions are satisfied.

Jurisdiction and International Law

Jurisdiction can become especially complicated when a case involves more than one country.

For example, a dispute may involve a person in one country, a company based in another country, and a contract performed in a third country.

Courts may then need to consider territorial jurisdiction, personal jurisdiction, and the interaction between different legal systems.

CARD 7 · HUMAN RIGHTS AND JUDICIAL REVIEW

Jurisdiction and Legal Protection

Jurisdiction and Human Rights

Jurisdiction can be connected to human rights because individuals may need access to a competent and independent court in order to have their legal rights properly determined.

The right to a fair hearing requires legal systems to provide appropriate courts or tribunals capable of deciding disputes according to law.

Jurisdiction and Judicial Review

Jurisdiction is closely connected to judicial review.

Courts may review whether a public authority has acted within the legal powers given to it.

If a public body acts outside the authority granted to it by law, this may be described as acting ultra vires, meaning "beyond the powers."

Jurisdiction helps establish the legal authority to decide a matter, while judicial review can examine whether public authorities have acted within the powers given to them by law.
CARD 8 · IMPORTANCE AND KEY TAKEAWAY

Why Jurisdiction Matters

Jurisdiction helps determine:

  • Which court can hear a case.
  • Which legal system applies.
  • Whether a court has authority over the parties.
  • Whether a decision will be legally binding.
  • Where a legal claim should be brought.
  • Which court can review or appeal a decision.

A case brought before a court without the required jurisdiction may be dismissed, transferred, or otherwise affected depending on the legal system and circumstances.
