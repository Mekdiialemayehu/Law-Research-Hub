CARD 1 · DEFINITION

Sources of Law

Sources of law are the places, authorities, and legal materials from which legal rules come.

They explain where laws originate and how courts and other legal institutions determine the rules that apply to a particular situation.

Different legal systems recognise different sources of law. The main sources can include constitutions, legislation, judicial decisions, customary law, treaties, and legal principles.

CARD 2 · CONSTITUTIONS & LEGISLATION

Main Sources of Law

1. Constitutions

A constitution establishes the fundamental principles and structures of a State. It usually explains how government is organised, defines the powers of public institutions, and may protect fundamental rights and freedoms.

In countries with written constitutions, ordinary laws generally must comply with constitutional requirements.

2. Legislation

Legislation consists of laws formally created by a legislative body, such as a parliament.

Legislation is one of the most important sources of law in many legal systems. It can create new legal rules, change existing rules, or remove rules that are no longer considered appropriate.

For example, a parliament may pass legislation regulating employment, education, taxation, or criminal offences.

CARD 3 · JUDICIAL DECISIONS & CUSTOM

Main Sources of Law

3. Judicial Decisions and Precedent

Courts interpret and apply the law when deciding cases. In legal systems that recognise the doctrine of precedent, decisions of higher courts can establish legal principles that lower courts must or may follow in later cases.

Judicial decisions can therefore play an important role in developing and clarifying the law.

4. Customary Law

Customary law develops from practices that have been followed over a long period and are recognised as legally binding within a particular legal system.

Customary rules may be especially important in legal systems where traditional practices form part of the recognised law.

CARD 4 · TREATIES & LEGAL PRINCIPLES

Main Sources of Law

5. Treaties and International Law

Treaties are formal agreements between States or other recognised international actors.

When a State becomes a party to a treaty, it may acquire international legal obligations. Treaties can regulate subjects such as human rights, international trade, refugees, and relations between States.

International law can therefore influence domestic legal systems, although the effect of treaties within national law depends on the country's constitutional and legal arrangements.

6. Legal Principles

Courts may also rely on broader principles of law when interpreting legislation or resolving legal questions.

Examples include principles such as fairness, equality, proportionality, legal certainty, and natural justice.

The role of these principles varies between legal systems.

CARD 5 · PRIMARY & SECONDARY SOURCES

Primary and Secondary Sources

Legal materials can also be described as primary or secondary sources.

Primary Sources

Primary sources are authoritative legal materials that establish or contain law.

  • Constitutions
  • Acts of Parliament
  • Regulations
  • Treaties
  • Judicial decisions

Secondary Sources

Secondary sources explain, analyse, or comment on the law rather than creating binding legal rules themselves.

  • Textbooks
  • Legal commentaries
  • Academic articles
  • Legal encyclopaedias
  • Research papers

Secondary sources can help students, lawyers, and judges understand legal principles, but their authority is generally different from that of primary legal sources.

CARD 6 · EXAMPLE

Sources of Law in Practice

Imagine a dispute concerning freedom of expression.

A court might examine the relevant constitutional provisions or human rights legislation first.

It may then consider previous judicial decisions interpreting those provisions.

The court could also consider an international human rights treaty where it is legally relevant within that legal system.

This demonstrates how several sources of law can work together when resolving a legal issue.

CARD 7 · IMPORTANCE & LEGAL SYSTEMS

Why Sources of Law Matter

Understanding the sources of law is essential because it helps determine:

  • Where a legal rule comes from.
  • Which legal authority has the power to create or change it.
  • Whether a rule is legally binding.
  • How courts should interpret and apply it.
  • Whether one legal rule has priority over another.

Without understanding sources of law, it would be difficult to determine which rules a court should apply in a particular case.

Sources of Law in Different Legal Systems

The importance of different sources varies depending on the legal tradition of a country.

In common-law systems, judicial precedent can have a particularly significant role. In civil-law systems, legislation and comprehensive legal codes are generally more central.

Many modern legal systems also combine different sources, including legislation, judicial decisions, constitutional rules, and international obligations.

CARD 8 · RELATED CONCEPTS & KEY TAKEAWAY

Key Takeaway

Sources of law are the recognised origins of legal rules. They can include constitutions, legislation, judicial decisions, customs, treaties, and legal principles.

Understanding where law comes from makes it easier to understand how legal rules are created, interpreted, and enforced.
