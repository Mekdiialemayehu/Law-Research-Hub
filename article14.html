Article 14 – Prohibition of Discrimination

A structured legal note on equality, non-discrimination, and the protection of individuals in the enjoyment of Convention rights.

Definition

Article 14 of the European Convention on Human Rights protects individuals from discrimination in the enjoyment of the rights and freedoms guaranteed by the Convention. It does not exist as a free-standing right on its own; instead, it applies in relation to another Convention right.

The provision prohibits discrimination on grounds such as sex, race, colour, language, religion, political or other opinion, national or social origin, association with a national minority, property, birth, or other status.

Legal Provision

Article 14 states that the enjoyment of the rights and freedoms set forth in the Convention shall be secured without discrimination on any ground such as sex, race, colour, language, religion, political or other opinion, national or social origin, association with a national minority, property, birth, or other status.

This means that when a person enjoys a Convention right, they must not be treated less favourably without an objective and reasonable justification.

Purpose of Article 14

The purpose of Article 14 is to ensure equality and prevent unfair treatment based on personal characteristics or status. It protects people from discriminatory treatment when they are enjoying Convention rights and helps promote fairness, equality, and respect for human dignity.

The Court’s case-law also shows that States may have positive obligations to address discrimination in practice, not just in theory.

Key Principles

  • No Independent Existence – Article 14 only applies together with another Convention right.
  • Broad Protection – The list of grounds is not closed, because “other status” can also be covered.
  • Direct Discrimination – A person must not be treated worse because of a protected ground.
  • Indirect Discrimination – A neutral rule can still be discriminatory if it has a disproportionate effect on a protected group.
  • Failure to Treat Differently When Required – Discrimination can also happen where persons in significantly different situations are treated the same without good reason.
  • Objective and Reasonable Justification – Different treatment may be allowed if there is a legitimate aim and a reasonable relationship of proportionality between the means used and the aim pursued.
  • Positive Obligation – In some situations, States must take action to protect people from discriminatory treatment.
  • Equality in Practice – Article 14 is about real equality, not only formal equality.

Leading Cases

  • Thlimmenos v. Greece – A leading Article 14 case. The Court held that discrimination can occur not only where people are treated differently without justification, but also where States fail to treat differently persons whose situations are significantly different.
  • D.H. and Others v. the Czech Republic – A major Grand Chamber case on indirect discrimination. The Court found a violation where Roma children were placed in “special schools,” showing that discriminatory impact matters even where the rule appears neutral.
  • Abdulaziz, Cabales and Balkandali v. the United Kingdom – A classic case on discrimination in family life and immigration, where the Court found discrimination on the ground of birth in conjunction with Article 8.
  • Molla Sali v. Greece – A leading case on discrimination and religion, where the Grand Chamber found a violation of Article 14 taken with Article 1 of Protocol No. 1 in a succession dispute involving the application of Sharia law.
  • Konstantin Markin v. Russia – A major gender equality case showing that Article 14 strongly protects equality between men and women.
  • Sampanis and Others v. Greece – A case showing the Court’s concern with discrimination affecting Roma children in education.

Key Legal Terms

  • Discrimination – Unfair treatment based on a protected characteristic or status.
  • Other Status – A flexible ground that can include many different personal circumstances.
  • Direct Discrimination – Less favourable treatment because of a protected ground.
  • Indirect Discrimination – A rule that seems neutral but affects one group more harshly.
  • Objective and Reasonable Justification – The legal test used to decide whether different treatment is allowed.
  • Proportionality – The balance between the aim of the measure and its effect on the individual.
  • Equality – The principle that people should be treated fairly and without unjustified difference.

Exam Tips

  • Remember that Article 14 is not standalone and must be linked to another Convention right.
  • Always identify the ground of discrimination and the Convention right involved.
  • Explain whether the treatment was different, and if so, whether there was an objective and reasonable justification.
  • Mention both direct and indirect discrimination where relevant.
  • Use cases such as Thlimmenos, D.H. and Others, Abdulaziz, and Molla Sali to support your answer.

Summary

Article 14 protects equality by prohibiting discrimination in the enjoyment of Convention rights. It is one of the Convention’s most important equality provisions because it covers a wide range of discriminatory treatment, including direct and indirect discrimination, and can require States to act where discrimination is occurring in practice.

Through its case law, the European Court of Human Rights has made clear that equality must be real, effective, and fair.
