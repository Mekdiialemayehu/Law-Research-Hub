ACCESS TO JUSTICE
● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ●
CARD 1 · DEFINITION

What Is Access to Justice?

Access to justice refers to the ability of individuals and groups to understand, use, and obtain a fair and effective legal remedy when their rights or interests are affected.

It answers the question:

Can a person actually obtain justice through the legal system?

Access to justice is an important principle of the Rule of Law and human rights.

Legal rights should not exist only in theory. People must also have a realistic opportunity to use courts, tribunals, legal procedures, or other appropriate mechanisms to protect those rights.

The exact meaning and scope of access to justice varies between legal systems.

CARD 2 · PURPOSE

Purpose of Access to Justice

The main purpose of access to justice is to ensure that people can seek legal protection and effective remedies when necessary.

It helps ensure that:

  • People can bring legal claims.
  • Individuals can defend themselves against legal accusations or claims.
  • Courts and other legal institutions are reasonably accessible.
  • Legal procedures are understandable and fair.
  • People can obtain legal assistance where necessary.
  • Unlawful government action can be challenged.
  • Legal rights can be effectively enforced.
  • Victims can seek appropriate remedies.
Access to justice connects legal rights with practical enforcement.
CARD 3 · RULE OF LAW

Access to Justice and the Rule of Law

Access to justice is closely connected to the Rule of Law.

The Rule of Law requires laws to be applied fairly and institutions to operate within legal limits.

However, legal rights have limited practical value if individuals cannot enforce them.

For example, a law may protect a person's right to property, but that protection becomes difficult to rely upon if the person cannot bring a claim before a court when the property is unlawfully taken.

Access to justice helps transform legal rights into enforceable rights.
CARD 4 · COURTS

Access to Courts

One of the most important aspects of access to justice is the ability to access courts.

A legal system should provide appropriate mechanisms through which individuals can:

  • Bring a claim.
  • Respond to a claim.
  • Challenge a government decision.
  • Defend themselves against criminal accusations.
  • Seek compensation or another remedy.
  • Appeal a decision where the law allows.
Access does not necessarily mean that every person must automatically win a case. It means that people should have a genuine opportunity to use the legal system according to applicable law.
CARD 5 · BARRIERS

Barriers to Access to Justice

Many factors can prevent people from effectively accessing justice.

  • High legal costs.
  • Court fees.
  • Lack of legal knowledge.
  • Lack of legal representation.
  • Geographic distance from courts.
  • Complex legal procedures.
  • Language barriers.
  • Disability or accessibility barriers.
  • Delays in legal proceedings.
  • Fear of authorities or retaliation.
  • Lack of information about available remedies.
These barriers may affect individuals differently depending on their circumstances.
CARD 6 · COST & LEGAL AID

Cost of Legal Proceedings and Legal Aid

Cost of Legal Proceedings

The cost of legal proceedings can be a major barrier to justice.

  • Lawyer's fees.
  • Court and filing fees.
  • Costs of obtaining documents.
  • Expert evidence.
  • Translation or interpretation.
  • Travel expenses.

Legal Aid

Legal aid refers to legal assistance provided to people who cannot afford private legal services, subject to the rules and eligibility requirements of the relevant legal system.

It may involve advice, legal representation, assistance preparing documents, or representation in particular types of proceedings.

CARD 7 · LEGAL REPRESENTATION & INFORMATION

Legal Representation and Legal Information

Legal Representation

Legal representation can make it easier for individuals to understand and use legal procedures.

  • Understand legal rights.
  • Identify possible legal remedies.
  • Prepare documents.
  • Present arguments.
  • Collect and organise evidence.
  • Follow procedural rules.
  • Respond to opposing arguments.

Legal Information

People must often understand the law before they can effectively use it.

Reliable legal information can help people understand their rights, responsibilities, remedies, procedures, deadlines, and where to obtain assistance.

CARD 8 · LANGUAGE & DISABILITY

Language, Disability and Accessibility

Language

Language can create a significant barrier to legal participation.

Depending on the legal system and circumstances, interpretation or translation services may be available.

Persons with Disabilities

Legal institutions should also be accessible to persons with disabilities.

  • Physical access to courts.
  • Accessible documents.
  • Communication assistance.
  • Interpreters where necessary.
  • Appropriate procedural accommodations.
  • Accessible digital services.
The broader principle is that disability or language should not prevent a person from effectively participating in legal proceedings.
CARD 9 · EQUALITY & VULNERABLE GROUPS

Access to Justice and Equality

Access to justice is connected to equality before the law.

If some people can easily use courts and legal remedies while others are effectively excluded because of financial, geographic, linguistic, or other barriers, legal rights may not operate equally in practice.

Equal access does not necessarily require identical treatment in every situation. Sometimes additional support or accommodation may be necessary to allow individuals to participate effectively.

Groups That May Face Greater Barriers

  • Children.
  • Persons with disabilities.
  • People living in remote areas.
  • People with limited financial resources.
  • People facing language barriers.
  • Victims of discrimination.
  • Persons affected by displacement or conflict.
CARD 10 · FAIR TRIAL RIGHTS

Access to Justice and Fair Trial Rights

Access to justice is closely related to fair trial rights.

A person must have a meaningful opportunity to participate in legal proceedings if a court is to provide a fair determination of their rights or obligations.

Fair trial protections may include:

  • Access to an independent and impartial tribunal.
  • Notice of proceedings.
  • The ability to present a case.
  • The ability to respond to opposing arguments.
  • Legal assistance where applicable.
  • A decision within a reasonable time.
  • Appropriate remedies and appeals.
CARD 11 · CRIMINAL & CIVIL JUSTICE

Access to Justice in Criminal and Civil Cases

Criminal Cases

Access to justice is particularly important in criminal proceedings because a person may face serious consequences, including imprisonment.

  • Access to a court.
  • The ability to understand the accusation.
  • Legal assistance where applicable.
  • The opportunity to present a defence.
  • The ability to challenge evidence.
  • An independent and impartial tribunal.
  • The right to appeal where provided by law.

Civil Cases

Civil justice allows individuals and organisations to resolve disputes involving contracts, property, employment, compensation, family matters, consumer disputes, and other legal interests.

CARD 12 · VICTIMS & ADR

Victims and Alternative Dispute Resolution

Access to Justice for Victims

Victims of crime and other unlawful conduct may require mechanisms that allow them to report wrongdoing, participate in proceedings where permitted, seek compensation, obtain protective measures, or obtain other appropriate remedies.

Alternative Dispute Resolution

Access to justice does not always require a dispute to be resolved through a traditional court trial.

  • Mediation.
  • Arbitration.
  • Negotiation.
  • Conciliation.
ADR can sometimes provide faster, less formal, or less expensive ways of resolving disputes, although its suitability depends on the type of dispute and applicable law.
CARD 13 · DELAYS & INDEPENDENCE

Delays and Judicial Independence

Delays

Justice can be weakened by excessive delays.

If cases take an unreasonable amount of time to resolve, individuals may struggle to obtain meaningful remedies.

  • Increased legal costs.
  • Loss of evidence.
  • Stress and uncertainty.
  • Prolonged disputes.
  • Reduced effectiveness of legal remedies.

Judicial Independence

People must be able to bring cases before courts that can make decisions independently from improper political or other external influence.

Judicial independence supports fair decision-making, impartiality, public confidence, protection of legal rights, and judicial review of government action.
CARD 14 · REMEDIES & HUMAN RIGHTS

Effective Remedies and Human Rights

Effective Remedies

Access to justice is closely connected to the availability of effective remedies.

  • Compensation.
  • Injunctions.
  • Declarations.
  • Reinstatement.
  • Judicial review.
  • Reversal of an unlawful decision.
  • Other remedies established by law.

Human Rights Law

Article 8 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights recognises the right to an effective remedy by competent national tribunals for acts violating fundamental rights.

Article 10 provides for a full and public hearing by an independent and impartial tribunal.

The International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights also contains provisions concerning effective remedies and fair hearings.

CARD 15 · GOVERNMENT & TECHNOLOGY

Government Accountability and Technology

Government Accountability

Access to justice allows individuals to challenge unlawful government action.

If a public authority acts outside its legal powers, an affected person may be able to seek judicial review or another legal remedy.

Access to justice helps ensure that government officials are also subject to the law.

Technology

Technology can both improve and create barriers to access to justice.

  • Finding legal information.
  • Filing documents.
  • Attending certain hearings remotely.
  • Communicating with legal institutions.
  • Tracking cases.

However, people without reliable internet access, digital skills, or suitable devices may face new barriers.

CARD 16 · EXAMPLE & KEY TAKEAWAY

Access to Justice in Practice

Imagine that a worker believes an employer has unlawfully withheld wages.

  1. Understand their legal rights.
  2. Find out which authority or court has jurisdiction.
  3. Obtain the required forms or documents.
  4. Pay any applicable fees or seek a fee waiver or legal aid.
  5. Present evidence supporting the claim.
  6. Participate in the proceedings.
  7. Obtain a legal decision.
  8. Seek an appeal or other remedy where permitted.

If the worker cannot afford legal assistance, does not understand the procedure, or cannot reach the relevant institution, the legal right to payment may be difficult to enforce.

Access to Justice = Ability to use the legal system + Fair and effective remedies.

Access to justice is not limited to having courts formally available. It also concerns whether people can realistically understand, enter, participate in, and obtain meaningful outcomes from legal processes.

Why Access to Justice Matters

  • Protects legal rights.
  • Provides effective remedies.
  • Promotes equality before the law.
  • Prevents arbitrary government action.
  • Resolves disputes peacefully.
  • Supports fair trial rights.
  • Holds authorities accountable.
  • Strengthens public confidence in the legal system.
  • Ensures legal rights can actually be enforced.
