Article 10 – Freedom of Expression

A structured legal note on freedom of expression and the protection of open debate under the European Convention on Human Rights.

Definition

Article 10 of the European Convention on Human Rights protects freedom of expression. This includes the freedom to hold opinions and to receive and impart information and ideas without interference by public authorities. It is a qualified right, which means expression can be restricted in limited circumstances, but only if the restriction is lawful, pursues a legitimate aim, and is necessary in a democratic society. The Court has repeatedly said that Article 10 is one of the essential foundations of a democratic society.

Legal Provision

Article 10 provides that everyone has the right to freedom of expression. This right includes freedom to hold opinions and to receive and impart information and ideas without interference by public authority and regardless of frontiers.

It also states that the exercise of these freedoms may be subject to such formalities, conditions, restrictions or penalties as are prescribed by law and are necessary in a democratic society for one of the legitimate aims listed in the Article, such as national security, territorial integrity, public safety, prevention of disorder or crime, protection of health or morals, protecting the reputation or rights of others, preventing disclosure of confidential information, or maintaining the authority and impartiality of the judiciary.

This means that Article 10 protects expression strongly, but it allows restrictions where they are lawful, pursue a legitimate aim, and are proportionate.

Purpose of Article 10

The purpose of Article 10 is to protect open debate, political discussion, journalism, criticism, artistic expression, and the exchange of ideas in a democratic society. It protects not only information or ideas that are accepted or harmless, but also those that may offend, shock, or disturb. The Court has also recognised that the press plays a vital role in a democratic society.

Key Principles

  • Freedom to Hold Opinions – A person has the right to hold opinions without interference.
  • Freedom to Receive and Impart Information – Article 10 protects both getting information and sharing it with others.
  • Qualified Right – Expression may be limited only under Article 10(2).
  • Lawfulness – Any restriction must be based on law.
  • Legitimate Aim – Restrictions must pursue one of the aims listed in Article 10(2).
  • Necessity in a Democratic Society – The Court asks whether the restriction is necessary and proportionate.
  • Protection of the Press – Journalism and public-interest reporting receive strong protection.
  • Offensive Speech May Still Be Protected – Expression is protected even when it offends, shocks, or disturbs.
  • Online Expression – Article 10 also applies to internet publication, comments, and digital communication.

Leading Cases

  • Handyside v. the United Kingdom – A classic Article 10 case. The Court held that freedom of expression protects views that may offend, shock, or disturb, and it became one of the most important foundations of Article 10 case law.
  • The Sunday Times v. the United Kingdom (No. 1) – Confirmed the importance of freedom of expression for public debate and the role of the press.
  • Lingens v. Austria – A leading case on criticism of public figures and the importance of political expression.
  • Animal Defenders International v. the United Kingdom – Confirmed that Article 10 can be restricted in some situations, but the Court must still carefully assess whether the restriction is justified.
  • Delfi AS v. Estonia – Showed that Article 10 applies to online news platforms and internet comments, and that liability for user-generated content can raise Article 10 issues.
  • Observer and Guardian v. the United Kingdom – A key case on press freedom and publication restrictions.

Key Legal Terms

  • Freedom of Expression – The right to express opinions and share information and ideas.
  • Opinion – A personal view or belief that is protected by Article 10.
  • Information and Ideas – Facts, messages, arguments, and viewpoints protected by the Article.
  • Interference – Any State action that limits expression.
  • Legitimate Aim – A reason allowed by Article 10(2), such as public safety or protecting the rights of others.
  • Proportionality – A test used to decide whether the restriction goes too far.
  • Public Debate – Discussion on matters of public concern, which is strongly protected.
  • Press Freedom – The freedom of journalists and media organisations to report and comment.

Exam Tips

  • Remember that Article 10 is a qualified right, not an absolute one.
  • Always ask whether there was an interference, whether it was lawful, whether it had a legitimate aim, and whether it was necessary and proportionate.
  • Mention that Article 10 protects speech that may offend, shock, or disturb.
  • Use cases such as Handyside, The Sunday Times, Lingens, and Delfi to support your answer.

Summary

Article 10 protects one of the most important freedoms in a democratic society: the right to express opinions, share information, and receive ideas. It protects political speech, journalism, criticism, and online expression, while allowing only limited restrictions that are lawful and proportionate. Through its case law, the European Court of Human Rights has made Article 10 a central safeguard for democracy, debate, and the free exchange of ideas.
