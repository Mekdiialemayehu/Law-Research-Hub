⚖️ Substantive vs. Procedural Law

SUBSTANTIVE VS. PROCEDURAL LAW
● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ●
CARD 1 · DEFINITION

Substantive vs. Procedural Law

Substantive law establishes legal rights, duties, obligations, and liabilities, while procedural law establishes the rules and processes used to enforce those rights and obligations.

Substantive law determines what the law considers lawful or unlawful and what rights and responsibilities individuals have.

Procedural law determines how legal claims are brought, how cases are conducted, and how courts and other legal institutions apply the law.

A simple way to remember the distinction is: substantive law concerns what the law provides, while procedural law concerns how the law is applied and enforced.
CARD 2 · SUBSTANTIVE LAW

Substantive Law

Substantive law creates and defines legal rights, duties, and obligations.

It can determine:

  • What conduct is prohibited.
  • What rights individuals have.
  • What obligations individuals or organisations have.
  • When a person may be legally responsible for harm.
  • What legal consequences may follow from particular conduct.

Areas of Law

Substantive law can be found in many areas of law, including:

  • Criminal law
  • Contract law
  • Tort law
  • Property law
  • Family law
  • Constitutional law
CARD 3 · CRIMINAL LAW

Substantive Criminal Law

Criminal law contains substantive rules that define criminal offences and establish the elements that must be proved.

For example, a criminal law may establish that a particular form of conduct constitutes theft and identify the requirements that must be satisfied before a person can be found guilty.

The rule defining the offence is substantive because it establishes what conduct is legally prohibited.

The substantive criminal rule therefore determines the nature of the offence and the legal conditions that must exist for criminal responsibility to arise.

CARD 4 · CONTRACT LAW

Substantive Contract Law

Contract law contains substantive rules concerning the rights and obligations of parties to legally binding agreements.

For example, if a business agrees to provide goods to a customer in exchange for payment, substantive contract law can determine what obligations each party has.

It can also determine what legal consequences may arise if one party breaches the agreement.

Substantive contract law therefore defines the legal rights and obligations created by a contract.
CARD 5 · TORT LAW

Substantive Tort Law

Substantive tort law establishes when one person may be legally responsible for causing harm to another.

Negligence is one important area of tort law.

A person who causes harm by failing to take reasonable care may be legally responsible for the resulting damage, depending on the applicable legal rules.

Substantive tort law determines when a civil wrong has occurred and when legal responsibility may arise.
CARD 6 · PROCEDURAL LAW

Procedural Law

Procedural law establishes the processes and rules through which legal rights and obligations are enforced.

It can regulate matters such as:

  • How a case is started.
  • How evidence is presented.
  • How parties must respond to legal claims.
  • How hearings are conducted.
  • How decisions are made.
  • How judgments may be appealed.
Procedural law is concerned primarily with how a legal dispute is resolved, rather than defining the underlying legal right or obligation itself.
CARD 7 · CIVIL PROCEDURE

Civil Procedure

Civil procedural rules govern how civil disputes are brought before courts and how those cases proceed.

For example, procedural law may establish how a claimant begins a lawsuit, how the defendant responds, how evidence is presented, and how the court reaches a decision.

It may regulate:

  • Starting a civil claim.
  • Serving legal documents.
  • Responding to claims.
  • Presenting evidence and arguments.
  • Conducting hearings.
  • Issuing and enforcing judgments.
CARD 8 · CRIMINAL PROCEDURE

Criminal Procedure

Criminal procedural law governs the process through which criminal cases are investigated, prosecuted, and decided.

Procedural rules may regulate:

  • Bringing charges.
  • Conducting criminal trials.
  • Presenting evidence.
  • Protecting the rights of accused persons.
  • Appealing a criminal conviction or sentence.
Criminal procedure focuses on the legal process used to determine whether a person should be held criminally responsible.
CARD 9 · KEY DIFFERENCES

Key Differences

Substantive Law Procedural Law
Establishes legal rights and duties Establishes the process for enforcing those rights and duties
Determines what conduct is lawful or unlawful Determines how legal proceedings are conducted
Defines offences and liabilities Governs investigations, hearings, trials, and appeals
Focuses on legal rights and obligations Focuses on legal procedures and processes
Answers largely: "What does the law require?" Answers largely: "How is the law applied?"
CARD 10 · EXAMPLE

Substantive vs. Procedural Law in Practice

Imagine that a person enters into a contract with a construction company to build a house.

Substantive Law

The substantive law of contract may establish that the company has a legal obligation to perform the work agreed upon and that the customer may have a legal remedy if the company breaches the contract.

Procedural Law

If the company fails to perform and the customer brings a claim before a court, procedural law determines how that claim is started and conducted.

It may establish how the parties submit documents, present evidence, attend hearings, and challenge the court's decision.

Substantive law: establishes the legal rights and obligations.

Procedural law: establishes the process for enforcing those rights and obligations.
CARD 11 · ACCESS TO JUSTICE

Relationship with Access to Justice

Procedural law is closely connected to access to justice.

If legal procedures are unclear, excessively complicated, or unfair, individuals may have difficulty enforcing rights that exist under substantive law.

Fair procedural rules help ensure that legal disputes can be resolved through an organised and impartial process.

Procedural Protections

  • The right to a fair hearing.
  • Equality before the law.
  • The right to present evidence.
  • The right to respond to allegations.
  • The right to appeal in appropriate circumstances.
CARD 12 · RELATIONSHIP & TAKEAWAY

How They Work Together

Substantive and procedural law work together.

Substantive law establishes the legal rules, rights, duties, and liabilities, while procedural law provides the mechanisms for applying and enforcing those rules.

For example, a law may give a person a legal right to compensation after suffering harm caused by another person's negligence. Procedural rules then determine how that person can bring a claim and how the court will decide it.

Substantive Law = What the law provides.

Procedural Law = How the law is applied and enforced.

Neither category operates completely independently. A legal system needs substantive rules to establish rights and obligations, but it also needs procedural rules to provide a fair and organised method of enforcing them.
