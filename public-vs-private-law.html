PUBLIC LAW VS. PRIVATE LAW
● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ● ●
CARD 1 · DEFINITION

Public Law vs. Private Law

Law is often divided into two broad categories: public law and private law.

Public law generally concerns the relationship between individuals and the State, as well as the organisation and exercise of government power.

Private law generally regulates legal relationships between individuals, businesses, and other private organisations.

The distinction helps explain what type of legal relationship is involved in a dispute.
CARD 2 · PUBLIC LAW

Public Law

Public law regulates the exercise of public power and the relationship between individuals and government institutions.

It includes areas such as:

  • Constitutional law
  • Administrative law
  • Criminal law
  • Human rights law
Public law is primarily concerned with government authority, public institutions, and the legal relationship between the State and individuals.
CARD 3 · CONSTITUTIONAL LAW

Constitutional Law

Constitutional law concerns the structure and powers of government and the fundamental principles of the legal system.

It can deal with issues such as the powers of Parliament, the executive, courts, and the protection of constitutional rights.

Key Areas

  • The organisation of government.
  • The powers of government institutions.
  • The relationship between branches of government.
  • The protection of constitutional rights.
  • Limits on government authority.
CARD 4 · ADMINISTRATIVE LAW

Administrative Law

Administrative law regulates decisions and actions taken by public authorities.

Government agencies and public officials must generally act within the powers given to them by law.

Example

A person may challenge a government agency's decision if they believe that the authority acted unlawfully or exceeded its legal powers.

Administrative law provides a way to examine whether public authorities have exercised their powers lawfully.
CARD 5 · CRIMINAL & HUMAN RIGHTS LAW

Criminal Law and Human Rights Law

Criminal Law

Criminal law is generally considered part of public law because criminal offences are treated as wrongs against society and are prosecuted by the State.

For example, when a person is charged with theft, the case is generally brought by a public prosecuting authority rather than by the victim personally.

Human Rights Law

Human rights law regulates the protection of fundamental rights and places legal obligations on States and public authorities.

Examples include protection of life, freedom from torture, freedom of expression, and equality.

CARD 6 · PRIVATE LAW

Private Law

Private law regulates relationships between private parties.

It includes areas such as:

  • Contract law
  • Tort law
  • Property law
  • Family law
Private law primarily governs legal rights and obligations between individuals, businesses, and other private organisations.
CARD 7 · AREAS OF PRIVATE LAW

Contract, Tort and Property Law

Contract Law

Contract law regulates legally binding agreements between parties.

For example, if a person agrees to buy a computer from a business and the business fails to deliver it after receiving payment, the buyer may have a contractual claim.

Tort Law

Tort law deals with civil wrongs that cause harm or loss.

Negligence is one important area of tort law. A person who causes harm by failing to take reasonable care may be legally responsible for the resulting damage.

Property Law

Property law regulates rights relating to ownership and possession of property.

It can govern matters such as buying, selling, using, and transferring property.

CARD 8 · KEY DIFFERENCES

Key Differences

Public Law Private Law
Concerns the State and public power Primarily concerns private relationships
Regulates public authorities Regulates individuals and organisations
Includes constitutional and administrative law Includes contract and tort law
Often involves questions of government authority Often involves disputes between private parties
Public bodies may be a central party Private individuals or organisations are usually the main parties
CARD 9 · EXAMPLE

Public Law vs. Private Law in Practice

Public Law Example

Imagine that a local government authority refuses to issue a permit.

A person challenging the lawfulness of that decision may be involved in a public law dispute because the case concerns the exercise of government power.

Private Law Example

Now imagine that the same person enters into a contract with a construction company, pays for work, and the company fails to perform the agreement.

That dispute would generally involve private law, specifically contract law.

The difference is the legal relationship involved: one concerns government power, while the other concerns a private contractual relationship.

CARD 10 · DISTINCTION & OVERLAP

Why the Distinction Matters

The distinction between public and private law helps determine:

  • What type of legal rules apply.
  • Which legal institutions may deal with the dispute.
  • What remedies may be available.
  • Whether the dispute concerns public power or a private legal relationship.

But the Boundary Can Overlap

The boundary between public and private law is not always completely separate.

A dispute between private individuals can sometimes involve public law issues, particularly where human rights or government regulation is involved.

Different areas of law can overlap, meaning that a single legal dispute may involve more than one area of law.
CARD 11 · HUMAN RIGHTS

Relationship with Human Rights

Human rights law is generally associated with public law because it places obligations on States and public authorities.

However, human rights principles can sometimes influence disputes between private parties, depending on the legal system and the particular right involved.

This demonstrates that different areas of law can interact and overlap rather than existing as completely separate categories.

CARD 12 · KEY TAKEAWAY

Key Takeaway

Public law focuses mainly on the State, public authorities, and the exercise of government power, while private law primarily regulates legal relationships between private parties.

Understanding this distinction helps identify the nature of a legal dispute and the rules that may apply.

It also provides a useful foundation for studying specific areas of law such as constitutional law, administrative law, criminal law, contract law, and tort law.
